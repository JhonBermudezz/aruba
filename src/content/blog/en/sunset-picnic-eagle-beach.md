Most of our picnics happen on Eagle Beach, and most of them start about two hours before sunset. This guide covers what we tell guests when they ask how to plan one: the sunset time for their month, when to arrive, what to bring and what we do when the weather turns.

## Eagle Beach faces the sunset

Eagle Beach sits on Aruba's west coast, so the sun goes down over the sea in front of your table. The sand is wide, which gives us room to set the teepee back from the water and away from other people. The trade winds blow from the east, and the west coast gets the calm side of the island.

We also set up on Surfside Beach, closer to Oranjestad, and on other beaches when guests ask.

## When to arrive

Arrive at the start time of your slot. Our team sets up before that, so the table is ready when you walk over, and we send you a map pin of the spot. Your picnic lasts two hours, and the end time stays the same if you arrive late.

The slot gives you about an hour and a half of daylight before the sun goes down. The light turns gold in the last half hour, which is when most guests take their photos. If you booked a photographer, that is when they work.

## What to bring

We bring the setup, the food, the drinks and the music speaker. Most guests bring:

- Sunglasses and sunscreen for the first hour, when the sun is still high.
- Sandals you can kick off. The table is low and you sit on cushions on the sand.
- A charged phone for photos and your playlist.
- Printed photos or a note, if you want us to add them to the table. Send them to us before the day.

Tell us about allergies when you book, so we can plan the board around them.

## If it rains

Rain is rare in Aruba, and most of it falls between October and January in short showers. If rain or strong wind would make your picnic unsafe, we offer you a new date or a full refund. A shower that passes before your start time does not cancel the picnic.
